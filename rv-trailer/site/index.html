The rule that decides everything

You can live on the land, in a registered trailer, once the house permit is issued

San Bernardino County's Temporary Structure Permit lets the person building a house live on the same lot in "a recreational vehicle or travel trailer" while they build.

  • Order: the house building permit is issued first, then this permit, then the trailer moves in. Putting it on the lot earlier can bring penalty fees and enforcement.
  • Term: 12 months, renewed 12 months at a time, never more than 5 years from the first issue. It lapses if no inspection is called within 180 days.
  • You submit: a plot plan, the trailer's current California DMV registration, and a sewage pumping contract if the trailer is self-contained. The final inspection covers the power, gas, sewer and water hookups.
  • Fees (from July 1, 2026): $301 to apply and $257 for each renewal; the schedule also lists $261 for a temporary RV site (ask whether both apply). A temporary power pole is $156. Each permit adds a $57 intake fee. Fees rise slightly on July 1, 2027 ($310, $264, $268, $160 and $58).
  • If the lot is inside the Town of Yucca Valley, the Town's code applies instead: a "self-contained recreational vehicle", 1 year plus 1-year extensions, 4 years at most, and it ends when the house is done. (Read from a third-party copy of the Town code; confirm with Town planning.)

So buy something the DMV registers: a travel trailer or a fifth wheel. State law counts a park model as an RV, but the County hasn't said it qualifies for this permit, so get that in writing before buying one. You can buy before the permit, but plan to keep the trailer at a storage lot or the dealer until the house permit is issued.

Your question

Can a small prebuilt pod count as storage?

Yes, if it is truly storage, and with the same rules as any shed. In the unincorporated County a storage building needs no building permit only if it is 120 sq ft or less, one story, under 14 ft tall, has no utilities, and sits inside the setbacks. Anything bigger needs an accessory-structure permit. Shipping containers and metal storage units are allowed in Rural Living if painted to blend in and placed behind the main building; their permit is $156 for one to three units, plus $195 plan review and a $57 intake fee.

  • Before the house exists, any accessory structure needs a Temporary Use Permit (County code 84.01.020(b)). Inside the Town of Yucca Valley, a storage building before the house is allowed only with a bond to remove it if the house isn't finished within 2 years.
  • Storage means storage. A pod with wiring needs an electrical permit, and one with a bed, insulation and air conditioning is a dwelling to an inspector, whatever it is called. Only the permitted trailer may be slept in; one complaint can bring an order to vacate. An imported capsule without a HUD, HCD or RVIA label can never become a legal bedroom or guest unit later.
  • Real cost. Capsules listed around $19,000-24,000 come to about $25,000-37,000 after import duty, and roughly $35,000-65,000 once freight, crane, foundation and hookups are added (estimates from the land research; no specific pod was priced here).

Better use of the $20,000: it buys a decent used trailer, the one interim home the County allows, and a 10 x 12 ft shed covers storage for a few thousand dollars more. If you love a particular pod, buy one with an HCD factory-built insignia or an RVIA park-model seal so it could become a legal guest unit later, get that in writing from the seller, and confirm with County Building and Safety before you buy.

Which trailer

What to buy for 1-3 years in the high desert

For two adults through 100F+ summers and freezing nights, aim for a 24-32 ft four-season travel trailer. Rooftop A/C only cools the air about 18-25F below outside, so shade matters more than the trailer.

How to buy

From shortlist to delivery

Paying cash or a short loan keeps you from owing more than the trailer is worth when you sell it. If you finance, RV lenders want 10-20% down; $50,000 over 15 years at 8.5% is about $492 a month and $38,600 in interest.

  1. ShortlistAgree on length, bed, budget cap and must-haves (dual-pane windows, heated tanks, 50 A, two A/Cs). Walk three or four floor plans at the Palm Desert dealer on a weekday.
  2. InspectDo your own walk-through for water damage first: press the floor at the door, bath and sink; sight down the walls for bubbles; check ceiling corners and roof seals. Then hire an NRVIA-certified inspector (find one on rvhelp.com; about $400-1,200 with travel).
  3. Price itAsk for an itemized out-the-door price: doc fee, prep, delivery, add-ons, tax and DMV. Sales tax is about 7.75% in unincorporated areas and 8.75% inside the Town of Yucca Valley, based on where the trailer will sit.
  4. Title and DMVTrailers over 16 ft are trailer coaches with yearly registration. Fees include $76 registration, a license fee of 0.65% of value and a transportation fee by value; run the DMV fee calculator with the VIN for the real total. Report the transfer within 10 days; the seller files a release within 5.
  5. Insure itA normal travel-trailer policy leaves liability to the tow vehicle, and you won't have one. Ask for a full-timer add-on (Progressive) or a stationary or full-timer trailer policy (Foremost writes these through agents; check it still does in California).
  6. Deliver itWrite dealer delivery and leveling into the deal, or book a tow-away driver (uShip averages about $3.39 a mile under 100 miles). Check that a truck and a 30-ft trailer can turn around on the lot.

Living in it

The desert checklist

HeatShade over the roof and the west side first. Two A/Cs on anything over 25 ft, 50 A service, a white reflective roof coating. Plan heavy cooking and laundry for the morning.
ColdSkirting with a small heater behind it, heated tanks, heat tape on the water line, and a 250-gal propane tank (a furnace can burn 1-3 gallons on a cold night).
WindBack end to the west wind, frame tie-downs with 48-in earth anchors, awning rolled up on windy days. Spring gusts reach 40-50 mph.
SunUV cracks roof sealant and tires even when parked: reseal each season, cover the tires, and take the weight off them with jack stands.
Dust and rodentsSeal every gap under the trailer with hardware cloth before the skirting goes on; check the wiring for chewing.
WaterTwo people use about 30-50 gallons a day, so a 1,550-gal tank lasts 30-50 days between deliveries.
SmellBlack tanks smell in the heat: keep them closed until two-thirds full, then dump all at once, and use enzyme treatment.
