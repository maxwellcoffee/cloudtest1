The rule that decides everything

You can live on the land in a registered trailer once the house permit, and then a trailer permit, are issued

San Bernardino County's Temporary Structure Permit lets the person building a house live on the same lot in "a recreational vehicle or travel trailer" while they build.

  • Order: the house building permit is issued first, then this permit, then the trailer moves in. Putting it on the lot earlier can bring penalty fees and enforcement.
  • Term: 12 months at a time. The County's 2025 checklist says renewals can continue up to 5 years from the first issue, but the Development Code text describes 12 months plus one 12-month extension, requested in writing at least 14 days before it expires. Get the County's answer in writing before planning a 3-year build. The permit lapses if no inspection is called within 180 days, ends if the house permit expires, and ends when the house is finished (the unit then has to be removed, within about 30 days).
  • You submit: a plot plan, the trailer's current California DMV registration, a sewage pumping contract if the trailer is self-contained, and will-serve letters from the water and sewer providers where they apply. The final inspection covers the power, gas, sewer and water hookups.
  • Fees (from July 1, 2026): budget about $570-830 for the first year: $301 to apply, possibly $261 more for a temporary RV site (ask whether both apply), $156 for the electrical permit for a temporary power pole, and $57 intake on each permit. Renewals are $257 a year. All rise by a few dollars on July 1, 2027.
  • If the lot is inside the Town of Yucca Valley, the Town's code applies instead: a "self-contained recreational vehicle" (its own fresh, gray and black tanks), 1 year plus 1-year extensions, 4 years at most, and it ends when the house is done. (Read from a third-party copy of the Town code; confirm with Town planning.)

So buy something the DMV registers: a travel trailer or a fifth wheel. State law counts a park model as an RV, but the County hasn't said it qualifies for this permit, so get that in writing before buying one.

Your question

Can a small prebuilt pod count as storage?

Yes, as storage only and under the same rules as a shed, but not right away. On a lot with no house yet, the County allows an accessory building only with a Temporary Use Permit (a separate permit from the trailer's; ask whether the two can be combined). Once the house is up, a pod of 120 sq ft or less, one story, under 14 ft tall, with no utilities and meeting the setbacks from property lines needs no building permit; anything bigger needs an accessory-structure permit.

  • Inside the Town of Yucca Valley, a storage building before the house is allowed only with a bond to remove it if the house isn't finished within 2 years.
  • Storage means storage. Add wiring and it needs an electrical permit. Add a bed, insulation or A/C and an inspector treats it as a home, whatever the seller calls it. Only the permitted trailer may be slept in, and one complaint can bring an order to vacate. An imported capsule without a HUD or HCD label can never become a legal bedroom.
  • Check the size. Many prebuilt pods sold as tiny homes are bigger than 120 sq ft (10 x 12 ft), so they need a full permit even as storage.
  • Shipping containers count as accessory structures too. Their County permit is $156 for one to three units, plus $195 plan review and a $57 intake fee; the land research found conditions such as matching paint and placement behind the house.
  • Heat. An unpowered box in a desert summer gets far hotter than the air. Keep electronics, photos and anything that can melt in a climate-controlled unit in town (listed from about $84-131 a month), which is also the simplest legal storage before the house permit.
  • Real cost. Imported capsules listed around $19,000-24,000 come to about $25,000-37,000 after import duty, and roughly $35,000-65,000 once freight, crane, foundation and hookups are added (estimates; no specific pod was priced).

Better use of the $20,000: it buys a decent used trailer, the one interim home the County allows; the setup around it, from water tank and skirting to permits, adds roughly $11,000-30,000 (see Tier A). If the $20,000 is on top of the trailer budget, a 10 x 12 ft shed or a used container covers storage for a few thousand dollars. If you love a particular pod, buy one with an HCD factory-built insignia (an RVIA park-model seal makes it an RV, not a dwelling), and ask County Building and Safety in writing whether it could become a legal guest unit later.

Which trailer

What to buy for 1-3 years in the high desert

For two adults through 100F+ summers and freezing nights, aim for a 24-32 ft four-season travel trailer. Rooftop A/C only cools the air passing through it by about 18-25F, and a single unit struggles above about 95F, so shade matters more than the trailer.

How to buy

From shortlist to delivery

Paying cash or a short loan keeps you from owing more than the trailer is worth when you sell it. If you finance, RV lenders want 10-20% down; $50,000 over 15 years at 8.5% is about $492 a month and $38,600 in interest.

  1. ShortlistAgree on length, bed, budget cap and must-haves (dual-pane windows, heated tanks, 50 A, two A/Cs). Walk three or four floor plans at the Palm Desert dealer on a weekday.
  2. InspectDo your own walk-through for water damage first: press the floor at the door, bath and sink; sight down the walls for bubbles; check ceiling corners and roof seals. Then hire an NRVIA-certified inspector (find one on rvhelp.com; about $400-1,200 with travel; an estimate, so get quotes).
  3. Price itAsk for an itemized out-the-door price: doc fee, prep, delivery, add-ons, tax and DMV. Sales tax is about 7.75% in unincorporated areas and 8.75% inside the Town of Yucca Valley, based on where the trailer will sit.
  4. Title and DMVAny trailer over 16 ft is registered every year. On a $50,000 trailer expect roughly $650 the first year ($76 plus 0.65% of the price plus smaller fees), less as it ages; the DMV fee calculator gives the exact total from the VIN. Report the transfer within 10 days; the seller files a release within 5.
  5. Insure itOrdinary trailer insurance relies on the tow vehicle's policy for liability, and you won't have a tow vehicle. Ask for a full-timer add-on (Progressive) or a stationary or full-timer trailer policy (Foremost writes these through agents; check it still does in California).
  6. Deliver itWrite dealer delivery and leveling into the deal, or book a tow-away driver (see the timeline below). Check that a truck and a 30-ft trailer can turn around on the lot.

Timing and logistics

From buying to selling

When to buy. The trailer permit application needs the trailer's California registration in your name, so you own it before you apply. The order is: house permit issued, buy and register the trailer, apply for the trailer permit, then delivery once it is issued. Buy as close to the house permit date as you can. Buying early to catch a year-end discount is fine if the dealer will hold it for a deposit or you keep it at an RV storage lot; add those months of storage, insurance and registration to the cost.

Getting it there with no truck. Write delivery to the lot and leveling into the dealer's price, and ask how far they deliver. For a private sale or a move out of storage, book a licensed tow-away driver. At uShip's short-haul average of about $3.39 a mile, that is roughly $150-350 from the dealers listed, before minimum charges. Expect the driver to check the brakes, lights, breakaway battery and tire dates, and to want the title or registration on board. Drive the dirt road to the lot first: soft sand and wash crossings are where deliveries get stuck. If you store it first, budget for two moves.

Keep three clocks running. Renew the trailer permit before each expiry. Keep the house permit active with regular inspections, because the trailer permit dies if the building permit expires. Renew the DMV registration every year even though the trailer never moves; the permit asks for current registration.

When the house is done. The trailer has to leave within about 30 days of the final inspection. Start the sale two or three months ahead: sell privately or consign it (Giant RV takes 2015-or-newer units). If you'd rather keep it as a camper, ask whether it can stay parked, unoccupied, on your lot; it can't become a guest room or rental. The solar, battery, water tank and shade stay and serve the house.

Why not a motorhome or a rental? A motorhome also counts as an RV, and you could drive it to the lot yourselves, but you pay for an engine that sits unused and still ages, and you get less room for the money. Renting long term runs an estimated $3,000-6,000 a month, more than buying and reselling over 1-3 years.

Inside. For two people full time, look for a rear-living or rear-kitchen plan with a big back window, a bed you can walk around, a real dinette or desk for work, a large fridge and the biggest tanks you can find. Skip bunkhouse plans. Spend an hour sitting in each shortlisted plan at the dealer.

Money

What two years really costs

Net cost is everything you spend (trailer, tax, setup, two years of running costs) minus what you get back: the trailer's resale and the solar, tanks and shade you reuse for the house. It uses the middle of each tier: about $40k, $93k and $193k up front, minus about $5k, $24k and $40k of equipment reused for the house.

Living in it

The desert checklist

HeatShade over the roof and the west side first. Two A/Cs on anything over 25 ft, 50 A service, a white reflective roof coating. Plan heavy cooking and laundry for the morning.
ColdSkirting with a small heater behind it, heated tanks, heat tape on the water line, and a 250-gal propane tank (a furnace can burn 1-3 gallons on a cold night).
WindBack end to the west wind, frame tie-downs with 48-in earth anchors, awning rolled up on windy days. Spring gusts reach 40-50 mph.
SunUV cracks roof sealant and tires even when parked: reseal each season, cover the tires, and take the weight off them with jack stands.
Dust and rodentsSeal every gap under the trailer with hardware cloth before the skirting goes on; check the wiring for chewing.
WaterTwo people use about 30-50 gallons a day, so a 1,550-gal tank lasts 30-50 days between deliveries.
SmellBlack tanks smell in the heat: keep them closed until two-thirds full, then dump all at once, and use enzyme treatment.
