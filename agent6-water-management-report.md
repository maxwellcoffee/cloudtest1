# Agent 6: Water Management & Fresh Water Delivery Report
## Japanese Wooden Hot Tub - No Chlorine / No Chemical Devices

---

## 1. Water Change Frequency by Scenario

### Scenario A: Salt Only, No Other Treatment
- **Frequency: Every 1-3 uses, or every 2-3 days with regular use**
- Salt alone (without an electrolysis/chlorine generator) provides minimal sanitization
- Pre-bathing shower/scrub (Japanese onsen tradition) can extend water life toward the 3-day end
- Without any sanitizer, watch for greasy film, cloudiness, or odor as signals to change
- Partial water changes (10-20%) between full changes can help slightly
- **Realistic assumption for cost modeling: every 2 days with regular use**

### Scenario B: Salt + UV or Ozone Sanitizer
- **Frequency: Every 5-7 days (weekly)**
- UV scrambles bacteria DNA; ozone destroys cell walls via oxygen radicals
- Neither provides a lasting residual sanitizer in the water, so water still degrades
- Ozone is poor at sanitizing tub walls/surfaces specifically
- Combining UV + ozone (AOP - Advanced Oxidation Process) is more effective than either alone
- Pre-bathing shower extends water life
- **Realistic assumption for cost modeling: every 7 days (weekly)**

### Scenario C: Salt + Hydrogen Peroxide (27% food-grade)
- **Frequency: Every 2-4 weeks**
- Hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) is a legitimate non-chlorine sanitizer that breaks down into water and oxygen
- Requires dosing every 3-4 days: ~1/3 cup per 100 gallons
- Maintenance dose: 1/8 cup per 100 gallons before each soak
- Must maintain ~100 ppm H2O2 level (use test strips)
- With diligent maintenance: water can last up to 4 weeks
- With casual maintenance or heavy use: closer to 2 weeks
- **Realistic assumption for cost modeling: every 14 days (2 weeks)**

### Summary Table: Water Changes Per Year

| Scenario | Change Frequency | Changes/Month | Changes/Year |
|----------|-----------------|---------------|--------------|
| A: Salt only | Every 2 days | 15 | 182 |
| B: Salt + UV/Ozone | Every 7 days | 4.3 | 52 |
| C: Salt + H2O2 | Every 14 days | 2.2 | 26 |

## 3. Fresh Water Delivery Services

### Do They Exist?
Yes. Bulk water delivery / water hauling services are widely available across the US. They primarily serve:
- Swimming pool fills
- Construction sites
- Livestock / agricultural needs
- Residential cisterns and wells
- Hot tubs and spas

### Typical Costs
| Item | Cost Range |
|------|-----------|
| Per gallon (delivered) | $0.03 - $0.10 |
| Per 1,000 gallons (delivered) | $30 - $100 |
| Minimum truckload delivery fee | $200 - $400 |
| Standard truckload (6,000 gal) | $375 - $600 |
| Small truck (1,750 gal) | $200 - $350 |

### The Problem for Hot Tub Owners
- Most water haulers have minimum delivery charges of $200-$400
- Their smallest trucks carry 1,750+ gallons
- For a 100-200 gallon hot tub fill, you would pay $200-$400 minimum for only 100-200 gallons actually used
- **This makes delivered water absurdly expensive for routine hot tub changes: $1.00 - $4.00 per gallon effective cost**
- Delivery services only make sense for locations without municipal water (wells, rural, off-grid)

### How to Find Local Water Delivery
Search terms:
- "bulk water delivery [your city]"
- "pool water delivery near me"
- "water hauling service [your area]"
- "residential water delivery [your county]"

Types of companies:
- Pool service companies (many offer fill services)
- Water hauling / trucking companies
- Fire department (some volunteer departments do pool fills as fundraisers)
- Landscape supply companies

## 5. Draining Considerations

### Where Does the Water Go?

Options for draining 100-200 gallons:
1. **Lawn / garden** - Best option IF water is low-salt or salt-free
2. **Gravel / landscape areas** - Good option for salt water
3. **Storm drain** - Check local regulations; generally permitted for non-toxic water
4. **Sanitary sewer cleanout** - Legal in most jurisdictions for gray water
5. **Dry well or French drain** - If you have one installed

### Salt Water Impact on Plants and Lawn

**This is the biggest concern with frequent salt water draining:**

- Salt does NOT evaporate from soil - it accumulates over time
- Salt dehydrates plant roots by drawing water away (reverse osmosis)
- Repeated salt water dumping will brown and kill grass
- Salt degrades soil structure and blocks nutrient absorption
- Even "low salt" concentrations become problematic with frequent application

**Impact by scenario frequency:**

| Scenario | Drains/Year | Salt Water on Lawn? |
|----------|-------------|---------------------|
| A: Every 2 days | 182 times | DEVASTATING - lawn will die within weeks |
| B: Weekly | 52 times | SEVERE - significant damage over a season |
| C: Every 2 weeks | 26 times | MODERATE to SEVERE - damage over months |

### Mitigation Strategies
1. **Use minimal salt** - only enough for desired feel, not a full salt system
2. **Rotate drain locations** - spread water across different areas
3. **Flush with fresh water** after draining salt water on an area
4. **Drain to hardscape** - gravel, driveway, non-planted areas
5. **Consider salt-tolerant plantings** near drain area
6. **Drain to sanitary sewer** if permitted locally
7. **Best option: keep salt concentration very low** (just enough for feel, not sanitization)

### Local Regulations
- Most municipalities allow draining non-chemically-treated water onto your own property
- Salt water discharge into storm drains may be regulated in some areas
- Some drought-prone areas (California, Arizona) have specific gray water reuse regulations
- Check with your local water utility or environmental department
- Hot tub water without chlorine/bromine is generally considered less problematic than chemically treated water

## 9. Key Recommendations

1. **Use municipal water via garden hose** - Water delivery services are not practical for frequent small fills. The minimum delivery charge ($200-$400) exceeds an entire year of municipal water in most scenarios.

2. **Scenario C (Salt + H2O2) is the best balance** - Fewest water changes, lowest water usage, reasonable consumable costs, and no equipment to buy/maintain.

3. **Scenario B is best if you want the least hands-on maintenance** - UV/ozone handles sanitization automatically; you just change water weekly and replace parts annually.

4. **Scenario A is viable but labor-intensive** - Water cost is still very low ($150-$290/year) but draining/refilling every 2 days is a significant chore and creates the most salt-water drainage problems.

5. **Minimize salt concentration** if you plan to drain on lawn/garden. Consider draining to hardscape, gravel, or sanitary sewer instead.

6. **Invest in an irrigation-only water meter** if your utility offers one - eliminates sewer charges on outdoor water use, cutting costs roughly in half.

7. **Pre-bathing ritual is essential** - Following the Japanese tradition of thorough washing before entering the tub can extend water life by 50-70% in any scenario.
