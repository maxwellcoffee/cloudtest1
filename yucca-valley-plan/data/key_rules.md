# Reconciled key rules (authoritative for every section of the page; researched 22-23 Oct 2026)

- Building permit life. County: work must start within 12 months of issuance, and the permit expires if work is suspended or abandoned for 12 months (County FAQ; state law HSC 18938.5-.6 / CRC R105.5.1 for permits since 2019). Town of Yucca Valley: its amended code also voids a permit 180 days after the last required inspection (read on a third-party code copy; ask the Town). Extensions of up to 180 days on written request for good cause; ask whether more than one is allowed. Working rule until confirmed in writing: pass an inspection at least every 5 months.
- Temporary living permit while building. County: only a DMV-registered RV or travel trailer, and only after the house's building permit is issued; 12-month terms renewable up to 5 years in total (County checklist Rev. 6/2025 and Code 85.15.080(f)); one older clause (84.25.040(e)) reads as 12 + 12 months, so get the County's answer in writing; the temporary permit also expires if no inspection is called within 180 days of issuance; remove the unit within 30 days of completion. Town: a manufactured home or self-contained RV, 1-year terms up to 4 years, only after building permits are issued. A park model may not qualify in the County (it needs current DMV registration; ask before buying). An uncertified imported capsule never qualifies in either place.
- Camping before any permit. County: owners of 5+ acre parcels in RL, AG or RC zones may camp up to 4 days in any 30 (ask whether days are counted consecutively or in total, and whether your zone suffix qualifies). Town: no camping allowance.
- Joshua trees. County Quick Reference Guide (Feb 2026), Acknowledgment form (rev 8-11-26) and grading checklist (May 2026): any land disturbance within 50 ft of a western Joshua tree (including trees on abutting parcels) needs CDFW approval first; that covers the house, ADUs, septic, trenches, fences, solar, sheds and driveways, and grading or brush clearing. Every stem counts as a tree. CDFW issues all permits (no county or town delegation yet): Region 6, r6cesa@wildlife.ca.gov, (909) 484-0167. 2026 in-lieu fees in the standard fee area, which includes the whole Morongo Basin: $354.75 (under 1 m), $521.75 (1-5 m), $2,609 (5 m+) per stem; CDFW must re-set fees by June 30, 2027. A project designed to fully avoid trees needs no CDFW permit. AB 1808 (Sept 2026) only helps owner-occupants of an existing house remove up to 10 trees near it.
- Wildlife. Mojave desert tortoise: state-endangered since July 15, 2025. Western burrowing owl: state candidate since Oct 2024. Bendire's and LeConte's thrashers: candidates since April 2026. Plan a biologist's survey before any clearing.
- Water. A new permitted house can't rely on hauled water (County Environmental Health citing Water Code 106.4; County code also requires a water purveyor where one can serve, or a well outside service areas). Hi-Desert Water District new service: $5,418 (5/8-inch sprinkler-ready service) or $10,836 (1-inch), plus a $200 fire-flow fee and installation at cost ($600 minimum). Bighorn-Desert View and Joshua Basin fees differ; get a written quote and a will-serve letter. A private well: roughly $25k-$100k+ depending on depth and yield, plus treatment if needed (estimates; get two driller quotes and nearby state well logs).
- Sewage. The septic ban covers only the Town's mapped sewer Phase 1 and Phase 2 areas (check the HDWD sewer map). Phase 2: the Basin Plan says Dec 31, 2028 (2031 only if the western Joshua tree is listed, a decision on hold until a 2033 review); HDWD says Phase 2 was extended to Dec 31, 2031. Plan on 2028 unless HDWD confirms 2031 in writing, and earlier if sewer reaches the lot; then connect within 180 days. Everywhere else (Town or County) you need a passing percolation (perc) test and a County-approved septic design; septic setbacks include 100 ft from a private well and 50 ft from a drainage course.
- Grading. County grading permit above 100 cubic yards of cut plus fill; hillside standards at 15%+ natural slope; soils engineer required above 20% slope or in fault, landslide or liquefaction zones; disturbing 1 acre or more needs a state stormwater permit; desert rules limit clearing to pads, driveways, landscaping and agriculture. Town: hillside review at 15%+ slope when grading exceeds 500 cy per lot or disturbance exceeds 10,000 sq ft.
- Fire access. Driveway at least 12 ft wide; up to 12% grade (gravel or native surface only up to 8%); turnaround if longer than 150 ft; fire access within 150 ft of all exterior walls; new houses need fire sprinklers, and so do later ADUs once the main house has them.
- Pods. County, 5+ acres: up to 3 accessory units in total (only one guest house; a junior ADU must be inside the house); detached ADU 200-1,200 sq ft, 18 ft tall; an ADU can't get its certificate of occupancy before the main house; ADUs of 500 sq ft or less avoid impact fees (under 500 sq ft avoids school fees). Up to 2 short-term-rental permits on parcels of 2+ acres, not for by-right ADUs, RVs or tents. Town: ADU rules not retrieved (ask Town Planning); short-term rentals capped at 10% of homes with a waitlist.
- Containers. County: accessory-structure permit (IB-0002, Jan 2026), painted to blend in, behind the main house, 5 ft from lot lines and 10 ft from buildings, out of the front setback, max 10 ft tall, no utilities or occupancy; on a lot with no house yet it also needs a Temporary Use Permit and a removal bond. Town: one container on RL lots of 2.5+ acres.
- House size. County: no minimum (state code needs one 70 sq ft room, a bathroom and a kitchen sink). Town: 725 sq ft minimum with 20-ft average width and two covered parking spaces (Planning Commission revisited this in mid-2026; ask the Town).
- Energy code (permits applied for from Jan 1, 2026). Heat-pump space and water heating are the baseline. Solar is required unless the formula gives under 1.8 kW; at 640 sq ft in Climate Zone 14 it gives about 1.73 kW, so solar is likely optional (confirm with a Title 24 consultant). Whole-floor electric radiant mats (about 3.4-4 kW) don't fit the "supplemental heat" exception (under 2 kW, on 30-minute timers), so the default for a whole soft heated floor is a hydronic slab fed by an air-to-water heat pump; electric mats only for small zones or after an energy model passes. A ductless mini-split handles cooling.
- Soft floor over radiant heat: keep everything above the heat at about R-1.4 (2.5 tog) or less; traditional 55-60 mm tatami blocks too much heat; use floor-heating tatami (15-30 mm), cork, or wool on a thin pad.
- Owner-builders may do their own electrical and plumbing under the permit; if you pay helpers you may be their employer (workers' comp); anyone you hire for a job over $1,000 must be licensed.
- Interim capsule cost: listings around $19k-24k become at least $25k-37k after 28-53% duties, and likely $35k-65k placed with freight, crane, foundation and hookups (estimates).
- Bhutan. Sustainable Development Fee (SDF): US$100 per adult per night, guaranteed only to Aug 31, 2027; after that Bhutan may return to the US$200 base rate (nothing announced). Visa US$40 one-time; tourist stays up to 90 days; a licensed guide is required for tourists (the official FAQ says at all times); tourists may only sleep in certified lodging. Budget about US$300-450 per person per day all-in; 2 weeks for two is about US$8,400-12,600 on the ground, about US$11,000-18,000 with flights. A personal-guest visa (a real Bhutanese friend who lives in Bhutan; reportedly 2 guests a year, 30 days max, SDF waived) is shorter, not longer, than a tourist stay; immigration rules unconfirmed because the immigration site was down. Longer stays: a genuine volunteer or work role with a registered organization, or the Gelephu Mindfulness City nomad residence (US$2,800/year + US$10,000 deposit; sold by a private operator; confirm with info@gmc.bt). Best seasons: March to mid-April, late Sept to Nov; monsoon late June to Sept.
- Timeline used on the page: Nov-Dec 2026 research and visits; Jan-Feb 2027 offer with a 45-60 day due-diligence period; Mar 2027 close; late Mar-Apr 2027 a 2-3 week Bhutan trip while the designer starts (belongings in a storage unit in town, since the lot has no permits yet); Apr-Aug 2027 survey, perc, biology, design and engineering, then submit plans; Sep-Dec 2027 plan check, permit, temporary trailer permit, move on, grade, septic, water, power, first natives in fall; 2028 build with an inspection at least every 5 months; late 2028-2029 final inspection; 2029 on: pods, orchard, and a longer Bhutan stay with nothing on a permit clock. A longer trip mid-build is possible only right after a passed inspection, under about 5 months, with any extension approved in writing first.
- Carrying costs during the build: about $5k-10k a year (property tax, insurance, fixed utility charges, permit renewals, storage), plus a caretaker while away.
