P-160 minutes, alone

Your solo hour

Five timed blocks that walk the five steps of David Allen's Natural Planning Model from Getting Things Done. This hour is for getting your own thoughts out of your head so you can listen well in the meeting, not for arriving with answers. Offer your wife the same hour if she'd like it. If not, keep your notes to yourself in the meeting until she has spoken.

Connecting worksheet…

Everything you type here saves as you go, to a worksheet you can both see. For anything you'd rather keep private for now, use a paper notebook.

Step 1 · Purpose + principles0:00 – 0:10 · 10 min

Why do I want this, and what are my standards?

  • Write the reason you want this land. Then ask "why does that matter?" up to four more times. Stop when you hit an answer that makes you feel something, and circle it.
  • Finish the sentence "I'd give someone else total freedom to run this project as long as they…" 5 to 7 times. These are your principles: the standards and boundaries you'd hold no matter who did the work.
  • Write your limits: what you won't do or risk. Examples, if they fit: borrow more than $___; live rough for more than ___ months; live in a pod the County could order removed; cut down a Joshua tree.
Step 2 · Outcome visioning0:10 – 0:20 · 10 min

See it going well

  • Write a postcard from October 2029 (5 min). Be concrete: what you hear at dawn, what the floor feels like, where the pods sit, what's growing, who is visiting.
  • Sketch the 5 acres on paper (5 min): road at the bottom, where the sun sets, where the wind comes from, where you'd want to sit.
  • If time is left, fill in the three snapshots.
Step 3 · Brainstorming0:20 – 0:38 · 18 min

Empty your head: about a minute per trigger

Write every question, worry, idea, person and errand that comes up. No judging and no organizing yet; volume is the goal. Give each trigger below about a minute, skip any that are empty, and spend leftover time on whichever ones you have the most to say about. Only after that, skim the considerations sheet to catch anything you missed.

Step 4 · Organizing0:38 – 0:50 · 12 min

Sort by stage, then mark who answers it

Sort everything into the stages you'll also use together: before an offer, during escrow (after an offer is accepted, before you close), before moving onto the land, while building, before Bhutan, later. Mark an item ★ if only the two of you can answer it, or ? if a professional should (County counter, surveyor, well driller, insurer). Step 5 · Next actions0:50 – 1:00 · 10 min

Decide the very next physical action

  • For the top five "before an offer" items, write the next thing you could physically do, starting with a verb. Tag it with where you'll be or what you'll need: @phone Call County Land Use Services and ask whether parcel number (APN) ___ is in the Town or the County.
  • Pick up to three fact-finding actions for this week: look-ups and phone calls only, nothing that commits money or contacts the seller. Put them on your next-actions list; use the calendar only for things tied to a specific day.
  • Ask your wife for a time when neither of you is tired, and send the note in the meeting sheet a few days ahead. A café in town and then a walk on the lot works better than writing on a phone in desert sun and wind.
P-290 minutes, together · one of you facilitates

Meeting together

The same five steps, run as a conversation. The facilitator protects the process: keeps time, makes sure you both write before you talk, and keeps decisions out of the brainstorm. Nothing about buying gets decided today; you're deciding what you need to find out. Ask whether your wife would like to facilitate a step, or the next session.

Connecting worksheet…

Send this a few days ahead

"I've been thinking a lot about the Yucca Valley land and I'd love to plan it with you properly, not just talk about it in passing. Could we find about 90 minutes this week, at a time and place that suit you? I'd like to use the five-step planning method from Getting Things Done: why we want this, what it would look like if it went well, everything we're excited or worried about, what comes first, and a few small next steps. We won't decide whether to buy anything. I've already spent an hour writing down my own thoughts; if you'd like to do the same, here's the page I used. If not, just bring whatever's on your mind, doubts included. And if you'd like to run it differently, tell me."

How to run it

Ground rules for two

  • Write first, then talk. For the big questions (why, principles, numbers), both of you write silently for 2 to 3 minutes before anyone speaks. Partner B chooses who goes first (B by default), so the facilitator's ideas don't set the frame.
  • Private answers go on paper first. Rankings and numbers: write them on paper, reveal at the same moment, then type them in. The worksheet is shared and saves as you type.
  • The facilitator shares last and writes the other person's points in their words, then reads them back: "Did I get that right?"
  • "Yes, and" while brainstorming. No evaluating until step 4. Worries count as ideas.
  • Sort every disagreement. Is it a fact (one of you finds out, by a date), a preference (take turns or trade), or a value (slow down, and each say what's underneath it)? If it still feels big once the facts are in, treat it as a value.
  • The timer serves you. Before moving on, ask "Anything else?" and wait. Either of you can add two minutes, or say "pause" with no reason needed; you breathe and stretch and nobody asks why. You can stop and finish another day.
  • A parking lot for anything off-topic. At the close, pick one item from it for your next weekly review.
Open0:00 – 0:05 · 5 min

Check in

Each of you, B first: How are you feeling right now? On a scale of 1 to 10, how much do you want this land today, and how nervous does it make you? Neither number is wrong and neither gets argued with. Then: what would make these 90 minutes worth it, and is there anything on the agenda you'd change or skip?

Step 1 · Purpose + principles0:05 – 0:20 · 15 min

Why this land, why now?

  • Write silently (3 min): "Why do I want this?" Share, B first. Listen for the overlap.
  • B drafts one shared purpose sentence from what you both said; A may suggest one change. Keep it short enough to remember on a hard day.
  • Principles (each writes for 3 min, then read aloud, B first): "We'll be happy with this project as long as…" and "I'd be unhappy if…". Write them as standards, not numbers, for example "we never borrow more than we could repay if the house never got built." Specific budget and risk numbers wait for step 4.
  • Note where you differ. You don't need to settle it now; put it in the parking lot.
Step 2 · Outcome visioning0:20 – 0:32 · 12 min

Tell each other what "going well" looks like

  • Each of you describes a day in October 2029 on the land, in the present tense, for two minutes, B first. The listener asks only "What else?"
  • Together, write down the moments from both stories that you'd both love.
  • Glance at the four site schemes only for what draws you ("I love the courtyard"), not to pick one. Choosing waits until you have survey facts.
Step 3 · Brainstorming0:32 – 0:50 · 18 min

Everything on the table

  • Silent write (8 min): each of you lists everything that comes to mind, using the triggers as prompts. About 30 seconds per trigger; skip any that are empty.
  • Round-robin (10 min): take turns reading one item at a time, B first, until both lists are empty. New ideas sparked by the other person are welcome. No discussing or fixing: the only reply is "Good, add it."
  • Whoever did the solo hour keeps those notes closed until the round-robin is finished, then adds anything still missing.

Step 5 · Next actions1:15 – 1:25 · 10 min

Three each, with dates

  • Each of you picks three next actions from the next-actions sheet or your own lists. Write each one as something you could physically do in one sitting, starting with a verb ("Call the water district and ask whether there's a meter at the road for parcel ___", not "figure out water"), plus the owner and a by-when date.
  • Book a first walk of the land with a local land agent. A surveyor usually comes in after your offer is accepted, during due diligence.
  • Note anything you're now waiting on from someone else, and since when.
  • Set a standing 30-minute weekly review (what moved, what's stuck, what's next) and take turns leading it.
Close1:25 – 1:30 · 5 min

Appreciate, then let it settle

Each of you, B first: one thing you appreciated about how the other showed up today, one thing you're excited about, and one worry you'll hold lightly. The listener says only "Thank you." Last question: is there anything we said today that you want to think about more before it counts? Pick one parking-lot item for the weekly review.

Bigger conversations for another evening

Questions that deserve their own time, ideally once the first facts are in. Good material for a second session on or near the land.
  • If we stop halfway (money, health, or we find we don't like desert life), what do we do with a part-built lot, and could we sell it?
  • Whose names go on the title, and how (joint tenancy, community property with right of survivorship, a trust)? What happens if one of us dies or we separate?
  • Is our money shared or separate, and if one of us puts in more, how do we record that?
  • How will we earn money while living there and building? Is the internet reliable enough for remote work? How many hours a week can each of us really give the build?
  • Who does what: physical labor, paperwork and inspections, dealing with contractors? What's the plan if one of us gets hurt?
  • How will two people share a tiny trailer, possibly for years: privacy, time alone, where each of us goes after an argument, showers and laundry?
  • Have we both spent a July heat wave and a windy winter night on or near the land? How far is the nearest hospital?
  • Does the plan still work if family life changes: children, ageing parents, pets, visitors?
  • What would make us pause or stop (costs 25% over, a year's slip), and on what dates will we formally re-decide?
  • What happens to where we live now, and when, relative to closing and moving onto the land?
  • What would each of us need to feel safe living there day to day: lighting, locks, cell coverage, a reliable vehicle, a plan for being alone on site?
  • Does either of us have a veto (spending over a set amount, legal risk), and is there a cooling-off rule before big commitments?
  • How will we know the build is straining us as a couple, and what will we do when it does?

The money hour

A separate 60-minute session, same rules: write first, reveal together, Partner B speaks first.
  1. 0:00 – 0:10 · Numbers on paper. Each writes three numbers privately: the most you'd spend in total, the reserve you won't touch, and the most you're comfortable carrying each month while you build. Reveal at the same moment. No arguing with a number, only "tell me more".
  2. 0:10 – 0:25 · Walk the line items in the money sheet. Mark each one known, estimate or unknown, and who will turn an unknown into a quote.
  3. 0:25 – 0:40 · Where the money comes from. Savings, sale or rent of where you live now, a home-equity line, cash flow during the build. Is it shared or separate, and how do you record it if one of you puts in more?
  4. 0:40 – 0:50 · Stop rules. What makes you pause and re-decide (for example costs 25% over, or a 6-month slip)? Is there a spending level where either of you can say no?
  5. 0:50 – 1:00 · Next actions. Three each, with dates. Put the stop rules into your "Our lot" walk-away field.
